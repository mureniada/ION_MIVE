# Adaptive Dialogue — Working Definition

> Status: draft. Authority: working material. Not an approved ION position.

## What Adaptive Dialogue is

Adaptive Dialogue in ION is a structured method of inquiry in which the form of the
conversation changes in response to the state of the evidence, rather than staying fixed
while only the words change. It is ION's answer to a specific failure mode: a dialogue that
sounds productive while the epistemic footing underneath it has quietly shifted. Where an
ordinary exchange treats every turn as the same kind of act, Adaptive Dialogue treats a turn
as one of several distinct kinds — establishing a question, gathering evidence, interpreting
that evidence, comparing interpretations, or recording what remains unresolved — and it
selects the next kind on the basis of what the previous turn actually established.

The adaptation is therefore epistemic, not stylistic. It is not tone-matching, persona
switching, or difficulty adjustment. Adaptive Dialogue does not adapt to who is asking. It
adapts to what is known, how strongly it is known, and where the current account is thin.

## Why it exists

ION holds that intelligence is not truth, and that retrieval is not reasoning. A system can
retrieve fluently, reason fluently, and still be wrong in a way that fluency conceals. A
fixed dialogue shape makes this worse, because it gives a weakly evidenced claim the same
conversational packaging as a well evidenced one. The listener has no signal to work with.

Adaptive Dialogue exists to keep that signal visible. When evidence is strong and
independent interpretations converge, the dialogue is permitted to move forward and
consolidate. When evidence is thin, contested, or supported by only one line of reasoning,
the dialogue is required to slow down, name the weakness, and return to gathering rather
than proceeding to conclusion. The shape of the conversation becomes a readable report on
the state of the evidence.

## Core commitments

Adaptive Dialogue inherits ION's invariants and adds no exemptions to them.

Independence is preserved. Where two interpretations are produced, neither may see the
other before comparison. Adaptive Dialogue governs the sequencing of turns; it never merges
two independent readings into one before they have been compared as separate objects.

Comparison does not become authorship. When interpretations disagree, the dialogue surfaces
the disagreement and its evidential basis. It does not silently replace two reports with a
smoother third one that no evidence supports.

Evidence outranks confidence. A confident turn with weak support is treated as a weak turn.
Adaptive Dialogue explicitly rejects the convention that assurance is a proxy for
reliability, because that convention is precisely what allows an unsupported claim to travel
further than a supported one.

Uncertainty is a first-class output. An Adaptive Dialogue that ends with a well-formed
statement of what remains unknown has succeeded. Suppressing that statement to produce a
cleaner ending is a failure, even when the resulting text reads better.

Degradation is visible. If only one line of reasoning is available where two were intended,
the dialogue must say so and must not present the single line as though comparison had
occurred. A one-model fallback is not a successful comparison.

## Relationship to the ION pipeline

Adaptive Dialogue is a layer of control over the existing chain, not a replacement for it.
The chain — question, retrieval, context pack, independent interpretation, comparison,
rendered answer with evidence and uncertainty — remains intact. What Adaptive Dialogue adds
is the decision, taken after each stage, about which stage should run next and whether the
dialogue has earned the right to advance at all. In a fixed pipeline that decision is
implicit and always the same. In Adaptive Dialogue it is explicit, recorded, and open to
review afterwards.

## Open questions

How adaptation decisions themselves should be evidenced is not yet settled. If the dialogue
can choose to slow down, that choice is an act with consequences, and it should be as
auditable as a retrieval or a comparison. The criteria that trigger a return to evidence
gathering are currently described qualitatively and have no agreed threshold. Whether
Adaptive Dialogue should ever terminate a line of inquiry outright, rather than returning it
with an uncertainty statement, is also unresolved.
